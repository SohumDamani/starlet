Key Findings

Conclusions
Finding 1 — Triage is effective
Triage reduces raw MVT tile size by 8.5–16.1% across all datasets and capacity levels. BG benefits most (richer attribute variety); TRACT and eBird see 8–11%.
Finding 2 — Gzip is the dominant compressor
Gzip alone reduces tile size by 47–70%. eBird (42 repeated string columns) compresses most aggressively; TIGER polygon data compresses less.
Finding 3 — Triage and gzip are complementary, not redundant
Triage adds a further 15–26% reduction on top of gzip (D vs B) across all datasets. Neither technique subsumes the other.
Finding 4 — Triage's marginal gain decreases as gzip strength increases
On attribute-heavy datasets where gzip is strongest (eBird, ~70%), triage's extra gain shrinks to ~15%. On lower-compression datasets (BG, ~48%), triage adds ~25%.
Finding 5 — Combined pipeline achieves 60–74% reduction vs raw
Triage + gzip (column D vs A) reduces tile size by 60–74% relative to raw MVT — the number relevant to real serving bandwidth and client decode time.
